# Comprehensive Guide to Inorganic Chemical Reactions

## 1. Acid-Base Reactions

### Principles
- **Brønsted-Lowry Definition**: Proton transfer from acid to base
- **Lewis Definition**: Electron pair donation from base to acid
- **Strong Acids**: HCl, HBr, HI, HNO₃, H₂SO₄, HClO₄
- **Strong Bases**: Group 1 hydroxides (LiOH, NaOH, KOH), Ba(OH)₂, Sr(OH)₂
- **Common Weak Acids**: HF, CH₃COOH, H₂CO₃, H₃PO₄
- **Common Weak Bases**: NH₃, amines, HCO₃⁻

### Reaction Patterns
- Strong acid + strong base → salt + water (complete neutralization)
- Strong acid + weak base → salt + weak acid species
- Weak acid + strong base → salt + weak base species
- Acidic oxide + base → salt + water (e.g., CO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O)
- Basic oxide + acid → salt + water (e.g., CaO + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O)

### Prediction Rules
- pH of resulting solution depends on relative strengths
- Gas formation occurs with carbonates: acid + carbonate → CO₂ + salt + water
- Amphoteric substances (Al(OH)₃, Zn(OH)₂) react with both acids and bases

## 2. Oxidation-Reduction (Redox) Reactions

### Principles
- **Oxidation**: Loss of electrons, increase in oxidation number
- **Reduction**: Gain of electrons, decrease in oxidation number
- **Common Oxidizing Agents**: Accept electrons (MnO₄⁻, Cr₂O₇²⁻, HNO₃, H₂O₂, O₂, Cl₂)
- **Common Reducing Agents**: Donate electrons (metals, I⁻, Fe²⁺, SO₃²⁻, C₂O₄²⁻)

### Common Redox Patterns

#### Metal Displacement
- Active metal + salt of less active metal → less active metal + salt of active metal
- Activity series: K > Na > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Sn > Pb > H > Cu > Ag > Au
- Example: Zn + CuSO₄ → Cu + ZnSO₄

#### Halogen Displacement
- More active halogen displaces less active halide
- Activity: F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂
- Example: Cl₂ + 2NaBr → 2NaCl + Br₂

#### Combustion
- Element + O₂ → oxide (often highly exothermic)
- Metals form basic oxides, nonmetals form acidic oxides

#### Decomposition Redox
- 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂ (catalyzed)
- 2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂ (heated with catalyst)

### Balancing Method
1. Assign oxidation numbers
2. Identify oxidized and reduced species
3. Write half-reactions
4. Balance atoms and charges
5. Combine half-reactions

## 3. Precipitation Reactions

### Principles
- Formation of insoluble solid from aqueous ions
- Driven by lattice energy exceeding hydration energy

### Solubility Rules
- **Always Soluble**: Group 1 salts, NH₄⁺ salts, nitrates, acetates
- **Mostly Soluble**: Chlorides, bromides, iodides (except Ag⁺, Pb²⁺, Hg₂²⁺)
- **Mostly Insoluble**: Carbonates, phosphates, sulfides, hydroxides (except Group 1, Ba²⁺, Sr²⁺, Ca²⁺)
- **Sulfates**: Soluble except Ba²⁺, Sr²⁺, Pb²⁺, Ca²⁺ (slightly)

### Prediction Algorithm
1. Identify all ions in reactants
2. Exchange cations and anions
3. Apply solubility rules to possible products
4. Precipitate forms if any product is insoluble

### Examples
- AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃(aq)
- BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2NaCl(aq)

## 4. Complexation/Coordination Reactions

### Principles
- Lewis acid-base reactions forming coordinate covalent bonds
- **Central atom**: Usually transition metal cation
- **Common Ligands**: Electron pair donors (H₂O, NH₃, CN⁻, Cl⁻, OH⁻, CO)

### Common Complex Types
- **Ammines**: [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺, [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺
- **Cyanides**: [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, [Ag(CN)₂]⁻
- **Halides**: [FeCl₄]⁻, [AlF₆]³⁻
- **Hydroxides**: [Al(OH)₄]⁻, [Zn(OH)₄]²⁻

### Stepwise Formation
- Complexes form in stepwise equilibrium
- Stability constants (Kf) determine predominance
- Chelate effect: multidentate ligands form more stable complexes

### Amphoteric Behavior
- Al³⁺ + 3OH⁻ → Al(OH)₃(s)
- Al(OH)₃(s) + OH⁻ → [Al(OH)₄]⁻(aq)
- Zn²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Zn(OH)₂(s)
- Zn(OH)₂(s) + 2OH⁻ → [Zn(OH)₄]²⁻(aq)

## 5. Thermal Decomposition Reactions

### Patterns by Compound Type

#### Carbonates
- Metal carbonate → metal oxide + CO₂
- Thermal stability: Group 1 > Group 2 > transition metals
- Example: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (above 900°C)

#### Hydroxides
- Metal hydroxide → metal oxide + H₂O
- Example: Ca(OH)₂ → CaO + H₂O (heated)

#### Nitrates
- Group 1 nitrates → nitrite + O₂ (except Li)
- Other metal nitrates → metal oxide + NO₂ + O₂
- Example: 2Cu(NO₃)₂ → 2CuO + 4NO₂ + O₂

#### Chlorates
- Metal chlorate → metal chloride + O₂
- Example: 2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂

## 6. Disproportionation Reactions

### Principles
- Same element both oxidized and reduced
- Requires intermediate oxidation states

### Common Examples
- Cl₂ + H₂O → HCl + HOCl (chlorine in 0 → -1 and +1)
- 2Cu⁺ → Cu + Cu²⁺ (copper(I) disproportionation)
- 3MnO₄²⁻ + 2H₂O → 2MnO₄⁻ + MnO₂ + 4OH⁻ (manganate)

## 7. Synthesis/Combination Reactions

### Patterns
- Metal + nonmetal → salt (e.g., 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl)
- Metal oxide + nonmetal oxide → salt (e.g., CaO + CO₂ → CaCO₃)
- Nonmetal oxide + water → acid (e.g., SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄)
- Metal oxide + water → base (e.g., CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂)

## 8. Gas-Forming Reactions

### Patterns
- Acid + carbonate → CO₂ + salt + water
- Acid + sulfite → SO₂ + salt + water
- Acid + sulfide → H₂S + salt
- Ammonium salt + strong base → NH₃ + salt + water

## Reaction Prediction Flowchart

1. **Identify reaction type**:
   - Metal + acid? → Redox (H₂ gas)
   - Metal + salt? → Single displacement
   - Two salts in solution? → Precipitation check
   - Acid + base? → Neutralization
   - Transition metal + ligand? → Complexation

2. **Check solubility** for precipitation

3. **Assign oxidation numbers** for redox

4. **Consider complex formation** with NH₃, CN⁻, OH⁻ excess

5. **Apply thermal stability rules** for decomposition

## Special Considerations

### Transition Metals
- Multiple oxidation states
- Color changes indicate redox or complexation
- Catalytic activity in some reactions

### Periodicity Trends
- Reactivity increases down Group 1, decreases down Group 17
- Oxide character: basic (left), amphoteric (center), acidic (right)

### Environmental Factors
- Temperature affects decomposition and equilibrium
- Concentration influences complex formation
- pH controls redox potentials and precipitation
