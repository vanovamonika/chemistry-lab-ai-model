# Inorganic Compound Reaction Matrix

## 1. ACID-BASE REACTIONS

### Strong Acid + Strong Base
**Reaction:** Complete neutralization
**Products:** Salt + Water
**Example:** HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
**Characteristics:** Highly exothermic, pH ≈ 7 at equivalence
**Observables:** Temperature increase, no visible change

### Strong Acid + Weak Base
**Reaction:** Partial neutralization  
**Products:** Salt + Weak acid species
**Example:** HCl + NH₃ → NH₄Cl
**Characteristics:** pH < 7, equilibrium favors products
**Observables:** Mild temperature change

### Weak Acid + Strong Base
**Reaction:** Partial neutralization
**Products:** Salt + Weak base species  
**Example:** CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O
**Characteristics:** pH > 7, equilibrium favors products
**Observables:** Mild temperature change

### Acidic Oxide + Basic Oxide
**Reaction:** Salt formation
**Products:** Salt
**Example:** CaO + CO₂ → CaCO₃
**Characteristics:** Solid-state reaction, may require heat
**Observables:** Solid mixture, possible color change

## 2. PRECIPITATION REACTIONS

### Soluble Salt + Soluble Salt
**Reaction:** Double displacement
**Products:** Precipitate + Soluble salt
**Example:** AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃
**Characteristics:** Driven by insoluble product formation
**Observables:** Cloudiness, solid formation, settling

### Factors Affecting Precipitation:
- **Solubility rules** determine if precipitate forms
- **Concentration** affects precipitation rate
- **Temperature** can change solubility
- **Common ion effect** reduces solubility

## 3. REDOX REACTIONS

### Active Metal + Acid
**Reaction:** Single displacement
**Products:** Salt + Hydrogen gas
**Example:** Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂↑
**Characteristics:** Oxidation number changes, exothermic
**Observables:** Gas bubbles, metal dissolution, temperature increase

### Metal + Salt Solution
**Reaction:** Metal displacement
**Products:** Different metal + Different salt
**Example:** Fe + CuSO₄ → Cu + FeSO₄
**Characteristics:** Activity series determines spontaneity
**Observables:** Color change, metal deposition, possible temperature change

### Halogen + Halide
**Reaction:** Halogen displacement  
**Products:** Different halide + Different halogen
**Example:** Cl₂ + 2NaBr → 2NaCl + Br₂
**Characteristics:** More reactive halogen displaces less reactive
**Observables:** Color change, possible gas evolution

## 4. COMPLEXATION REACTIONS

### Transition Metal + Ligand
**Reaction:** Coordination complex formation
**Products:** Coordination compound
**Example:** Cu²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺
**Characteristics:** Color changes, geometry changes
**Observables:** Dramatic color change, dissolution of precipitates

### Amphoteric Hydroxide + Base
**Reaction:** Complex ion formation
**Products:** Soluble complex
**Example:** Al(OH)₃ + OH⁻ → [Al(OH)₄]⁻
**Characteristics:** pH-dependent solubility
**Observables:** Precipitate dissolves in excess base

## 5. GAS-FORMING REACTIONS

### Carbonate/Hydrogen Carbonate + Acid
**Reaction:** Acid decomposition
**Products:** CO₂ gas + Salt + Water
**Example:** CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + CO₂↑ + H₂O
**Characteristics:** Vigorous bubbling
**Observables:** Gas evolution, possible effervescence

### Sulfite + Acid
**Reaction:** Acid decomposition  
**Products:** SO₂ gas + Salt + Water
**Example:** Na₂SO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + SO₂↑ + H₂O
**Characteristics:** Pungent gas formation
**Observables:** Gas with characteristic odor

### Sulfide + Acid
**Reaction:** Acid decomposition
**Products:** H₂S gas + Salt
**Example:** FeS + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂S↑
**Characteristics:** Rotten egg odor
**Observables:** Gas with strong odor

### Ammonium Salt + Strong Base
**Reaction:** Base decomposition
**Products:** NH₃ gas + Salt + Water
**Example:** NH₄Cl + NaOH → NaCl + NH₃↑ + H₂O
**Characteristics:** Pungent alkaline gas
**Observables:** Gas evolution, basic vapor

## 6. DECOMPOSITION REACTIONS

### Thermal Decomposition Patterns:

#### Carbonates
**General:** MCO₃ → MO + CO₂↑
**Example:** CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂↑
**Conditions:** Heating required
**Observables:** Gas evolution, mass loss

#### Hydroxides
**General:** M(OH)₂ → MO + H₂O↑
**Example:** Ca(OH)₂ → CaO + H₂O↑  
**Conditions:** Moderate heating
**Observables:** Water vapor, solid remains

#### Nitrates
**Group 1:** 2MNO₃ → 2MNO₂ + O₂↑ (except Li)
**Others:** 2M(NO₃)₂ → 2MO + 4NO₂↑ + O₂↑
**Observables:** Brown NO₂ gas, oxygen

#### Chlorates
**General:** 2MClO₃ → 2MCl + 3O₂↑
**Example:** 2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂↑
**Observables:** Vigorous oxygen evolution

## 7. COMBINATION/SYNTHESIS REACTIONS

### Metal + Nonmetal
**Reaction:** Direct combination
**Products:** Binary compound
**Example:** 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl
**Characteristics:** Often highly exothermic
**Observables:** Heat, light, flame possible

### Metal Oxide + Nonmetal Oxide
**Reaction:** Salt formation
**Products:** Oxy-salt
**Example:** CaO + CO₂ → CaCO₃
**Characteristics:** Acid-base reaction of oxides
**Observables:** Solid formation

### Nonmetal Oxide + Water
**Reaction:** Acid formation
**Products:** Oxy-acid
**Example:** SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄
**Characteristics:** Highly exothermic
**Observables:** Heat, acid formation

## 8. DISPROPORTIONATION REACTIONS

### Halogens in Alkaline Solution
**Reaction:** Self-oxidation-reduction
**Products:** Halide + Hypohalite
**Example:** Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaOCl + H₂O
**Characteristics:** Same element oxidized and reduced
**Observables:** Color change of halogen

### Hydrogen Peroxide
**Reaction:** Self-decomposition
**Products:** Water + Oxygen
**Example:** 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂↑
**Characteristics:** Catalyzed decomposition
**Observables:** Oxygen gas evolution

## 9. HYDROLYSIS REACTIONS

### Salt of Weak Acid/Base + Water
**Reaction:** Hydrolysis
**Products:** Weak acid/base + H⁺/OH⁻
**Example:** CH₃COONa + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COOH + NaOH
**Characteristics:** pH changes from 7
**Observables:** Solution becomes basic/acidic

## REACTION PREDICTION FLOWCHART

### Step 1: Identify Compound Types
- **Metal + Acid** → Redox (H₂ gas)
- **Metal + Salt** → Single displacement  
- **Two Salts** → Precipitation check
- **Acid + Base** → Neutralization
- **Carbonate + Acid** → Gas formation
- **Transition Metal + Ligand** → Complexation

### Step 2: Check Driving Forces
- **Precipitation** (insoluble product)
- **Gas Formation** (volatile product)  
- **Weak Electrolyte** (water, weak acid/base)
- **Redox** (oxidation number changes)

### Step 3: Apply Specific Rules
- **Solubility rules** for precipitation
- **Activity series** for metal displacement
- **Acid/base strength** for neutralization
- **Ligand field strength** for complexation

## SPECIAL CASES AND EXCEPTIONS

### Passivation
- **Aluminum, Chromium** form protective oxide layers
- **Concentrated HNO₃** passivates iron, aluminum

### Amphoteric Behavior
- **Al, Zn, Sn, Pb** hydroxides dissolve in excess base
- Forms complex ions: [Al(OH)₄]⁻, [Zn(OH)₄]²⁻

### Catalyst Effects
- **MnO₂** catalyzes H₂O₂ decomposition
- **Pt** catalyzes SO₂ to SO₃ oxidation

### Temperature Dependence
- **NH₄Cl** decomposition reversible with temperature
- **Ca(OH)₂** solubility decreases with temperature

This comprehensive guide enables systematic prediction of inorganic reactions based on compound types and reaction patterns.
